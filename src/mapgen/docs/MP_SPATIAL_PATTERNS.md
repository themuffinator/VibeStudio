# Multiplayer spatial patterns — 13 September 2026

Build compactness through shared encounter volumes, nearby route junctions and selective overlap between floors. Leave enough open space for fighting, sightline separation and recognizable landmarks. Filling a plan's blank pixels is not a design objective.

This study combines the six-map local Quake Live atlas (`out/ql-atlas/index.html`, written by `python -B -m tools.ql_atlas.package`), supplementary **Deep Inside**, and two Quake II archive references. Exact local paths, SHA-256 hashes, source contracts and evidence classifications are in [the research record](../knowledge/mp/spatial-patterns.json). The QL inputs are the `QL-preSteam` directory snapshot, all IBSP47; their release dates and equivalence to current Steam revisions are unverified. Authored mode filters are retained, not evaluated. These are examples to reason from, not new quality scores or capacity labels.

The atlas uses actual model-zero draw triangles and sampled quadratic patches, with sky/nodraw excluded. Height slabs are clipped before projection. Its surfaces include trim, roofs, fog surfaces and inaccessible geometry; some entity geometry is omitted. A rendered surface is not proof of a floor, support, headroom or a navigable connection. Supplied package levelshots were inspected for spatial/theme context; they are not new engine renders and their exact runtime association was not tested. Nothing from these maps was copied into generated geometry or assets, and no model weights or profile partitions changed.

## The verified local crate comparison

The compiled Q2 world has 589 player-blocking brushes under mask `SOLID | WINDOW | PLAYERCLIP | MONSTER` (`33619971`). There are no embedded `func_*` or `trigger_*` entities in this reference. Its authored teleporter pads lie outside the local test bounds. The crate base is `[-544,-160,-128]..[-480,-96,-64]`; the upper crate is offset to `[-560,-160,-64]..[-496,-96,0]`. Surface 2618 carries a box texture on the upper top. The base, rather than the upper silhouette, blocks the standing crossing.

Both routes start at XY(-528,-192), end at(-528,-64), and keep the player origin Z −104, feet Z −128. West uses(-592,-192)→(-592,-64); east uses(-448,-192)→(-448,-64). All eight recorded poses and six segments pass exact stored-plane checks with a 32×32×56 hull and zero collision epsilon. Support brush 286 contains every endpoint bottom footprint, and its convexity covers the intervening flat segments. The direct hull sweep hits brush 207. A world point ray between the same XY endpoints at eye Z −82 first hits at fraction 0.25. These are two certified local static detours, not a shortest-path, whole-map connectivity, native trajectory or tactical balance result.

The author's readme recommends 2–5 players while archive metadata says 3–5, and reports an interior stair bug. That historical bug was not reproduced here. Keep the conflict and limitation instead of silently upgrading the map to a flawless training example. Existing **Violent & Funky** (`gladdm1`) water brush 64 is also recorded, but its water exit is not certified; the earlier [drop/ordinary-return proof](Q2_DROP_RETURN_STUDY.md) concerns a different height region.

## Quake II contracts for the next blockout

The exact local original-Q2 files and hashes are in the record. They describe that source snapshot; the active game module still needs native verification.

* **Space:** the standing player hull is 32×32×56, with origin 24 above the feet; the step limit is 18. A 144–160-unit candidate walkway is a project design choice, not a learned QL standard. At an upper floor 128 above the lower floor, subtract the actual deck thickness when checking underpass height; nominal floor separation does not establish clearance. Sweep corners, approaches, crossovers and the complete usable surface.
* **Depth:** `PM_CategorizePosition` samples a standing player at floor+1,+23,+46. With the bed as support and no other contents,20-unit water normally reaches only the first sample;24 or44 reaches the second and invokes swimming. Crouching changes these samples to +1, +11, +22, so the same 24-unit water can fully submerge the crouched view. Test entry, stationary movement, forward movement and crouching separately.
* **Water motion:** `PM_WaterMove` halves clamped wish speed, applies water acceleration, and includes currents; `PM_Friction` scales water friction by water level. This does not mean every measured velocity or travel time is exactly half its dry value. The special waterjump checks a solid point 30 ahead, then empty space 16 higher; it does not guarantee arbitrary lips are escapable. Use explicit, hull-checked exits and test them in the game.
* **Liquid cost:** water entry/exit and submersion transitions emit sounds. Full submersion can exhaust air; the inspected source resets air to 12 seconds while not fully underwater and applies escalating drowning damage after depletion. Slime/lava damage scales with immersion and equipment; damage-per-invocation is not a universal DPS. Falling damage depends on motion state and immersion, so water volume and drop height cannot prove a harmless landing.
* **Teleport arrival:** stock `misc_teleporter` has a 64×64×8 solid pad and a 16×16×16 source trigger above it. `teleporter_touch` clears velocity, sets the destination orientation, applies the teleport hold, places the player at destination origin+10Z, and invokes `KillBox`. Test occupied arrival, repeated arrivals, hull/headroom, floor contact and first movement. Do not transfer QL momentum behavior or use a target point alone as an arrival certificate.

For the proposed **Fold** experiment, a shallow 24-unit water lane therefore means a swimming-state cost under the standing assumptions above. Partial 128-high walkways, five close spaces, a horseshoe and an optional teleporter are experiment choices. This research does not claim any final Fold compile, runtime, route or playtest outcome.

## Compactness and validation

Across these six QL records, upward rendered XY union area divided by its own bounding rectangle ranges from about 0.492 to 0.732. This is **projected surface coverage**, not playable density, exterior dead-space fraction, room occupancy or a quality ranking. It includes inaccessible tops and can merge unrelated overlapping floors; its complement includes meaningful courts and occlusion gaps. The QL denominator also differs from an authored Q2 room-envelope denominator, so those ratios cannot be compared directly.

Use these separate artifacts before judging a compact layout:

1. A route-role sketch: two or more independent circuits, distinct approaches to major contests, and an ordinary graph with special edges disabled. Record why each upper route and each intentional void exists.
2. Per-floor support plans and compiled hull sweeps, including the complete selected walkways, barrier detours, water exits and teleporter arrival. Distinguish horizontal clearance, floor support and eye/projectile sightlines.
3. A region-use plan that labels fighting area, circulation, item commitment, deliberate visibility void, solid separator and inaccessible exterior. Measure authored space separately from the atlas surface proxy.
4. Matched native runs and human playtests for journey time, lost speed, route choice, control of entrances, post-arrival deaths and resource access. A shorter graph distance alone does not establish tighter flow.

Change one feature per comparison: remove one upper walkway; fill only one horseshoe inset; remove one barrier; drain only the optional water lane while keeping its bed; disable only the teleporter; or shorten one exterior connector while keeping doorway angles fixed. Preserve spawn/item configuration and intended player count. Watch for the useful tradeoff disappearing, one route monopolizing play, new spawn exposure, or compactness making every encounter audible/visible from the same location.

This approach agrees with Patrick Williams' advice to provide multiple approaches, distribute resources and use teleporters deliberately, while allowing risky dead ends for specific purposes. That is expert guidance, not a measurement of these map revisions. [Multiplayer Level Design Guide](https://robotrenegade.com/articles/multiplayer-level-design-guide.html). The Level Design Book similarly treats flow as the movement a design asks for and requires blockout/playtesting to verify its feel. [Flow](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/layout/flow).
